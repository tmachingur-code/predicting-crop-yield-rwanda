Our mission

Yields differ a lot between plots, even for the same crop. We turn survey data into a simple view of where plots fall short and what goes with it, so limited support such as advice, seed, fertilizer and soil conservation can go where it matters most.

Who we are

Predicting Crop Yield in Rwanda is a machine learning project that uses the national Seasonal Agriculture Survey to estimate how a plot's yield compares with the typical plot of the same crop.

Our purpose: to help the people who support farmers see where plots are likely to fall short and which conditions go with it, so decisions about advice, inputs and soil conservation rest on evidence from real survey data.

How it works

1

Real survey data

National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda survey, Season A 2021/22. Three tables are merged plot by plot.

2

Compare with typical

For each plot we ask how far its yield is above or below the typical plot of the same crop. 1.25 means 25% above typical.

3

Learn the patterns

A gradient boosting model learns from location, plot size, soil erosion, fertilizer, seed and more. It is tested on survey areas it has never seen.

4

Ask about a plot

Describe a plot below and get its expected yield, compared with a typical plot of that crop.

Location comes first

District is the biggest factor for all three crops. For maize, fertilizer per hectare, seed spending and plot size come next.

Soil erosion

Less erosion goes with higher yield in the data, but it matters little once location is known.

Limits

  • One survey season (2021/22). This describes a normal season and is not a forecast.
  • The results are associations, not proof of cause. Plots that use fertilizer may differ in other ways.
  • Scores are modest: the survey has no weather data, so much of the difference between plots stays unexplained.
  • Next steps: add 2020/21 for a year-to-year test, and rainfall by district.
